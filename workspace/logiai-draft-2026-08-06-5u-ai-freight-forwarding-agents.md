# The People Who Run the Forwarders Just Funded the Software That Automates Them

*A Munich startup raised $3.2 million in pre-seed to put AI agents on the freight-forwarding desk. The round is small. The cap table is the story.*

The official version is a modest one: 5U AI, founded in 2025 by two Technical University of Munich graduates, closed a $3.2 million pre-seed on 28 July 2026, led by London's Emerge Capital. In a year where supply-chain AI rounds routinely clear nine figures, this barely registers.

Read the backer list and it registers differently. Standing behind 5U AI are senior operators from DHL, GEODIS, DSV, Maersk and Ceva Logistics. That is not a generic logistics-angel roster. Those are the five names a European shipper reads off its own carrier contracts. When the people who spent careers running forwarding operations write checks into the software that automates the forwarding desk, the signal is not about $3.2 million. It is about where they think the desk is going.

## What 5U AI actually builds

5U AI calls its product a "digital workforce": multi-agent AI workers that handle quoting, bookings, shipment tracking, data entry and routine administration across air, sea and road freight. The framing matters. This is not another visibility dashboard or a chatbot bolted onto a TMS. It is agents doing the operational tasks that today sit with a human forwarding clerk, the ones that fill a working day with copy-paste between a carrier portal, an email thread and an operational system.

That is the same category thesis we saw with Auger and with Augment's "Augie": the unit of automation is the task, not the report. The difference here is scope and geography. 5U AI is not chasing the Fortune 100 planning stack. It is aimed squarely at European freight forwarders, the mid-market operators who move air, sea and road freight and whose margins live and die on how many shipments one clerk can process without an error.

## Why the round size is the wrong number to watch

Three point two million buys a seed team and a runway, not a market. The reason to pay attention is the validation structure, not the capital.

A pre-seed backed by DHL and DSV operators tells you the incumbents' own people believe the clerk-level task is automatable now, not in a 2030 roadmap. It also tells you those incumbents would rather have a stake in the tooling than be surprised by it. For a mid-market forwarder without a DHL-scale R&D budget, that is the useful read: the automation of the operational desk is no longer a question of whether, but of build, buy or partner, and the largest players are already choosing "invest."

## What to watch over the next twelve months

- **A named production customer, not a pilot.** The claim is agents doing real forwarding tasks. The evidence is a European forwarder running quotes or bookings through 5U AI in production, with a volume number attached.
- **Error rate, not task count.** Forwarding automation only pays if the agent's booking is right often enough to trust unsupervised. Watch for a published accuracy or exception-rate figure, the metric every one of these platforms is quiet about early.
- **Whether the backers become buyers.** The tell is if any of DHL, DSV, GEODIS, Maersk or Ceva moves from cap-table to customer. An operator who invests is interested. An operator who deploys has decided.

The $3.2 million will be forgotten by the time this plays out. What will not be is the pattern: the operating leaders of the largest European forwarders putting their own money into the software that thins out the forwarding desk. For a mid-market forwarder, the near-term action is small and specific. At your next operations review, count how many hours a week your team spends moving data between a carrier portal and your TMS. That number is exactly the surface 5U AI, and the people funding it, are aiming at.
